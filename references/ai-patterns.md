# AI writing patterns, with Turkmen examples

Every pattern here is the "safe default" a language model picks because it fits the widest range of readers. A person writes for one reader and one subject, so their choices are uneven and specific. That unevenness is what you are restoring.

Two rules run through the whole file:

- Every sentence you keep must give the reader something new.
- One weak tell proves nothing. People use dashes, signposts and lists too. Act on a weak tell only when others appear in the same passage. Strong tells (marked **strong**) justify an edit on one sighting.

## 1. Chatbot leftovers (strong)

Greetings, praise of the question, offers of more help, "I hope this helps". They were written for a chat window, not for the reader of the final text. Delete the wrapper, keep the content.

Watch for: `Elbetde!`, `Hökman!`, `Gowy sorag!`, `Ine, siziň üçin ...`, `Size kömek edip bilsem şat bolaryn`, `Umyt edýärin, bu size kömek eder`, `Başga soragyňyz bar bolsa, ýazyň`, `Islän wagtyňyz ýüz tutuň`, and the Russian/English equivalents (`Конечно!`, `Надеюсь, это поможет`, `Certainly!`).

> Before: Elbetde! Ine, gysgaça maglumat: Mary welaýatynda pagta ýygymy tamamlandy. Umyt edýärin, bu peýdaly bolar!
>
> After: Mary welaýatynda pagta ýygymy tamamlandy.

## 2. Announcing instead of stating (strong)

The sentence says that a point is coming, or that it is important, instead of making it.

Watch for: `Şuny bellemek gerek, ...`, `Aýratyn bellemeli zat, ...`, `Geliň, ... seredeliň`, `Indi ... geçeliň`, `Ýokarda agzalanlardan görnüşi ýaly`, `Bu ýerde möhüm bir zady ýatlatmak isleýärin`.

> Before: Şuny bellemek gerek, täze ýol 2027-nji ýylda ulanylmaga beriler.
>
> After: Täze ýol 2027-nji ýylda ulanylmaga beriler.

> Before: Geliň, bu meselä jikme-jik seredeliň. Ilki bilen, tarifler barada.
>
> After: Tarifler üç topara bölünýär. (start with the content)

## 3. Empty contrast: "not only X, but Y"

`diňe bir X däl, eýsem Y`, `X däl-de, Y`, `bu jedel däl, bu hakykat`. The first half names something nobody claimed, so the second half sounds bigger. State the point directly. Keep the contrast when both halves carry information or the first half corrects a belief the reader really holds.

**Register note:** in Turkmen official and news prose `diňe bir ... däl, eýsem ...` is a normal, frequent construction. In a news item, keep it unless it appears in nearly every paragraph. In a blog post, letter or social post, rewrite it.

> Before: Bu programma diňe bir tekst ýazmak üçin däl, eýsem tablisa düzmek üçin hem ulanylýar.
>
> After: Bu programmada tekst hem ýazyp bolýar, tablisa hem düzüp bolýar.

## 4. Repeating closers and summary paragraphs

A one-line paragraph that repeats the paragraph before it, or a final paragraph starting `Netijede`, `Jemläp aýtsak`, `Gysgaça aýtsak` that adds nothing new. In a long report a real conclusion is fine. In a short text, end on the last concrete fact.

Watch also for a sentence that explains an example the reader just saw: `Bu bolsa ... uly mümkinçilik`, `Munuň özi ... aýdyň subutnamasydyr`.

> Before: Kitaphana sişenbe günleri hem işleýär. Bu bolsa okyjylar üçin uly mümkinçilik.
>
> After: Kitaphana sişenbe günleri hem işleýär.

## 5. Forced threes

Ideas come in threes because three sounds complete, not because the meaning has three parts: `ösüşi, abadançylygy we rowaçlygy`, `bilim, medeniýet we sport`. Check that each item adds a distinct idea. Keep three when the facts are three.

**Register note:** official Turkmen prose uses long enumerations on purpose. In news, keep enumerations that list real things (ministries, regions, events). Cut abstract triads in any register.

> Before: Festiwal medeniýeti, dostlugy we hyzmatdaşlygy berkitmäge gönükdirilendir.
>
> After: Festiwalyň maksady ýurtlaryň arasynda medeni gatnaşyklary ösdürmek.

## 6. Inflation, clichés and sales language

An ordinary fact is dressed as a turning point or a gift.

Watch for: `täze sahypa açýar`, `möhüm ädim`, `ýol açýar`, `aýratyn ähmiýete eýe`, `uly orun eýeleýär` (when nothing says why), `ajaýyp mümkinçilik`, `gaýtalanmajak`, `deňsiz-taýsyz`, `misli görülmedik` (outside ceremonial formulas), `geljegi nurly`, `täze belentliklere`.

Keep the fact, drop the dressing. If the source says what changed, say that instead.

> Before: Täze köpri şäheriň taryhynda täze sahypa açýan möhüm ädimdir.
>
> After: Şäherde täze köpri guruldy. (add what it connects only if the source says so)

## 7. Bureaucratic padding

Models stack verbal nouns and helper verbs where one verb would do: `amala aşyrylmagy`, `ýerine ýetirilmegi`, `-ýandygyny bellemek bolar`, `durmuşa geçirilmegi maksadyna laýyklykda`. Turkmen allows long chains, and official documents use some of them, but a chain that says nothing is padding in any register.

> Before: Taslamanyň durmuşa geçirilmegi maksadyna laýyklykda degişli işleriň amala aşyrylýandygyny bellemek gerek.
>
> After: Taslama boýunça işler dowam edýär.

Prefer the plain predicate: `bar`, `ýok`, `-dyr/-dir`, `edýär`, `açyldy`, `geçirildi`.

## 8. Connector chains and same openings

Nearly every sentence starts with a linking phrase: `Şeýle hem`, `Mundan başga`, `Bulardan başga`, `Bu bolsa`, `Şonuň üçin`, `Netijede`, `Ýagny`, `Hususan-da`. Or several sentences in a row start with the same subject. People let most sentences follow on without a connector. Keep a connector where it shows a real relation (cause, contrast, sequence) and delete the rest. Merge sentences that start the same way.

> Before: Mekdepde sport zaly bar. Şeýle hem, ýüzüş howzy bar. Mundan başga, kitaphana hem bar.
>
> After: Mekdepde sport zaly, ýüzüş howzy we kitaphana bar.

## 9. Vague authority

`Hünärmenleriň pikiriçe`, `Barlaglar görkezýär`, `Köp adamlar şeýle hasaplaýar` with no one named. If the source names the expert or study, name them. If not, drop the borrowed authority and state the claim with the certainty the source supports.

> Before: Hünärmenleriň pikiriçe, bu usul has netijeli.
>
> After: (if the source names them) Oba hojalyk institutynyň alymlary bu usulyň hasyly artdyrýandygyny aýdýarlar. (if not) Bu usul has netijeli bolup biler.

## 10. Knowledge-limit disclaimers (strong)

`Elimdäki maglumatlara görä`, `Soňky täzelenmä çenli`, `Bu barada jikme-jik maglumat ýok, ýöne ähtimal ...`. The model talks about its own knowledge, then fills the gap with a guess. State what the source does not show, or delete the sentence. Never keep the guess.

## 11. Formatting by reflex

- Bold labels with colons on every list item (`**Esasy pikir:** ...`). Turn the list into sentences when the labels carry no information.
- Headings on a text of three paragraphs.
- Emoji in anything except a social post that already uses them.
- Long dashes (—) in every other sentence. Turkmen does use the dash, for specific jobs: dialogue, a missing linking verb between subject and predicate, an explanatory insertion. Everywhere else use a comma, colon or full stop. If the user's own sample text uses dashes, keep its rate.
- `"..."` or `“...”` instead of `«...»`.

## 12. Uniform rhythm

Every sentence of similar length and shape, every paragraph three sentences long. Read the text aloud in your head. Merge two short sentences, split a long one, let a paragraph be one sentence when the point is one sentence.

## When not to act

- Quotations, official names, titles of documents and laws, and fixed formulas of the genre stay as they are, even if they contain a "tell".
- Salutations and closings of a letter (`Hormatly ...`, `Hormat bilen, ...`) are part of the letter, not chatbot residue.
- A specific, unusual detail, a real opinion, a doubt, a joke or an aside is the writer's voice. Keep it.
- Text written before late 2022 was not written by a chatbot. Edit it for grammar only, if asked.
- Several tells together are the evidence. A single dash or a single `Şeýle hem` is not.
