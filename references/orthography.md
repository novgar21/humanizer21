# Turkmen orthography for editors

Rules are summarized in our own words from the orthography and phonetics pages of enedilim.com. Points marked *(usage)* are standard practice that those pages do not cover; check them in the orthographic dictionary when it matters.

Contents: 1 alphabet · 2 letters · 3 vowel harmony · 4 consonant changes before suffixes · 5 dropped vowels · 6 loanwords · 7 hyphens, abbreviations, numbers · 8 compound words · 9 capital letters · 10 line breaks · 11 checklist

---

## 1. Alphabet

30 letters: 9 vowels and 21 consonants.

- Vowels: `a e ä i o ö u ü y`
- Consonants: `b ç d f g h j ž k l m n ň p r s ş t w ý z`
- Order: `A B Ç D E Ä F G H I J Ž K L M N Ň O Ö P R S Ş T U Ü W Y Ý Z`

Letters that do not exist in Turkmen, and what to write instead:

| Wrong | Right | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `ı` (Turkish dotless i) | `y` | `kız → gyz`, `yıl → ýyl` |
| `ğ` | `g` (covers both sounds) | `ağız → agyz`, `çağa → çaga` |
| `v` | `w` | `waza`, `teplowoz` |
| `x` | `h` | `biohimiýa` |
| `c` (Turkish /dʒ/) | `j` | `jaý`, `eje` |
| `ñ`, `ng` | `ň` | `müň`, `soň` |
| `ts` for Russian ц | `s` | `stansiýa` |

No apostrophe before a suffix, unlike Turkish: `Aşgabat'da → Aşgabatda`, `Meret'iň → Merediň`.

## 2. Letters

### 2.1 `y` (vowel) and `ý` (consonant)

The most common machine error.

- `y` is a back unrounded vowel (like Turkish ı, Russian ы): `gyz`, `ylym`, `yssy`, `yşyk`.
- `ý` is a consonant (Russian й, English "y" in "yes"). It can stand anywhere: `ýüz`, `ýigit`, `baýrak`, `geýim`, `baý`, `aý`.
- Wrong → right: `yol → ýol`, `yyl → ýyl`, `yigit → ýigit`, `gelyar → gelýär`, `bay → baý`.
- Russian я, ю, ё, е after a vowel or at the start of a word: `ýa`, `ýu`, `ýo`, `ýe` *(usage)*: `ýanwar`, `ýubileý`, `informasiýa`.
- Practical test: a `y` directly followed by another vowel is almost always a mistake for `ý`.

### 2.2 Long vowels

Length is not marked; `ak` (white) and `ak` (flows) look the same.

- Long `y`/`i` at the end of a word is written with `ý`: `gyý`, `iý`, `diý`; native words keep it before suffixes: `iýmek`, `diýmek`.
- Long `y`/`i` inside a word gets nothing: `miwe` (not miýwe), `nire`, `gyz`, `tiz`.
- Long `ü` in a first syllable is written `üý`: `süýt`, `güýç`, `çüýşe`, `tüýdük`.
- Long `u` at the end of a word is written `uw`: `suw`, `guw`; a few words take `ý`: `duý`, `guý`.

### 2.3 `ä` and `e`

Separate letters; the choice depends on the word: `bäş`, `şäher`, `zähmet`, `däne` but `sen`, `demir`, `ýedi`. Several front suffixes contain `ä`, and machines often write `e`:

- `-ýär`: `gelýer → gelýär`, `gözleýer → gözleýär`
- `-räk`: `tizrek → tizräk`
- `-däki`: `Meretdeki → Meretdäki`

### 2.4 `o`/`ö` only in the first syllable

In simple native words `o` and `ö` appear only in the first syllable. Later syllables have `a` and `e`: `oglan` (not oglon), `owal`, `börek` (not börök), `gözenek`.

Exceptions: compounds (`Gündogar`, `günorta`, `ýolgörkeziji`, `Daşhowuz`), the suffixes `-sow/-söw`, `-hor`, `-hon` (`kitaphon`, `parahor`), pairs with `bi-` (`sorag-bisorag`), and Russian loans (`traktor`, `telefon`).

### 2.5 `u`/`ü` up to the second syllable

In simple words `u` and `ü` stand in the first and second syllables; from the third on, write `y`/`i`: `gurluşyk`, `üzümçilik`, `guşçulyk`.

### 2.6 Consonants with position limits

- `ň` never starts a word: `goňur`, `müň`, `soň`.
- `b`, `d`, `j` do not end native words; loans may (`gerb`, `rekord`, `kollej`).
- `ž` mainly in loans: `žurnal`, `žanr`, `bagaž`.
- `w` is native (`gawun`, `düwme`) and also stands for Russian в.
- `g` and `h` each cover several sounds, so `ğ` and `x` are never needed.

## 3. Vowel harmony

| Front (inçe) | Back (ýogyn) |
|---|---|
| `e ä i ö ü` | `a o u y` |

### 3.1 Front/back harmony

Native words keep all vowels front or all back. Suffixes choose their variant from the last vowel of the stem:

| Back stem | Front stem |
|---|---|
| `-lar`, `-da`, `-dan`, `-dy`, `-ly`, `-lyk`, `-çy`, `-ny`, `-nyň`, `-ym`, `-mak`, `-ýar`, `-rak`, `-daky` | `-ler`, `-de`, `-den`, `-di`, `-li`, `-lik`, `-çi`, `-ni`, `-niň`, `-im`, `-mek`, `-ýär`, `-räk`, `-däki` |

- `balyk → balykçylar`, `iş → işçileriň`, `oba → obadan`, `öý → öýümizde`.
- Wrong → right: `kitaplerde → kitaplarda`, `öýlarda → öýlerde`, `geldy → geldi`, `kompýuterlar → kompýuterler`.
- Mixed-vowel stems (loans, compounds) follow the last syllable: `pionerler`, `direktorymyz`, `telefonda`, `kitabyň`.
- Stems ending in `-sk` take front suffixes with a linking `i`: `Krasnowodskiniň`, `Krasnowodskide`.
- Loanwords that break harmony keep their spelling: `kitap`, `esas`, `emma`, `edebiýat`, `mähriban`, `kärhana`, `telefon`, `radio`.
- Fixed-form elements ignore harmony: `-hana`, `-dar`, `-kär`, `-nama`, `-stan`, `-baz`, `-ban`, `-zada`, prefixes `bi-`, `bed-/bet-`, `nä-`: `erkana`, `betbagt`, `mähriban`.

### 3.2 Rounding harmony

- After a rounded first syllable, a short narrow vowel in the second syllable is rounded: `ogul` (not ogyl), `üzüm` (not üzim), `goýun`, `kömür`, `öküz`.
- Long narrow vowels later in the word stay unrounded: `uzyn`, `üçimiz`.
- A narrow vowel at the end of a two-syllable word stays unrounded: `uly`, `tokly`, `goşgy`, `gözi`, `böri`. It rounds when a consonant-initial suffix follows: `goşgular`.
- Next to `w`, narrow vowels are rounded: `gawun`, `ýazuw`, `hyjuw`.
- Suffixes `-uw/-üw`, `-wuk/-wük`, `-ewünt` are always rounded: `ýazuw`, `berüw`, `düşewünt`.

## 4. Consonant changes before suffixes

### 4.1 `k p t ç` → `g b d j`

Applies to stems of two or more syllables and to one-syllable stems with a long vowel, before a vowel-initial suffix (and before `-rak/-räk`, `-ýar/-ýär`):

- `kitap → kitaby`, `taýak → taýagy`, `orak → oragyň`, `elek → elege`
- `agaç → agajy`, `güýç → güýjüň`, `Meret → Merediň`, `giç → gijräk`
- verbs: `howluk → howlugýar`
- Wrong → right: `kitapy → kitaby`, `agaçy → agajy`

No change:

- one-syllable stems with a short vowel: `it → itiň`, `top → topuň`, `at → atýar`, `aç → açýar`, `gep → gepi`
- Arabic/Persian loans in `-at/-et/-ýat` and some others: `syýasaty`, `hökümeti`, `milleti`, `medeniýeti`, `edebiýaty`, `zähmeti`, `niýeti`, `tarapy`
- Russian loans ending in `p`, `t`, `ç`: `teleskopy`, `instituty` (exception: `peç → pejiň`); final `k` in Russian loans usually changes: `tehnigi`, `akademigi`

These short stems change anyway: `et`, `git`, `aýt`, `gaýt`, `gurp`, `harç`, `gant`, `köşk`: `edýärin`, `gidýärsiň`, `aýdýarsyň`.

Across a word boundary nothing changes in writing, whatever the pronunciation: `gök ot`, `içip otyr`, `ak öýli`.

### 4.2 Suffixes starting with `d`

After `z`, `l`, `n`, `s` write `d`, even if you hear `t` or a doubled consonant: `bizde`, `aldy`, `ýoldaş`, `menden`, `Nepesden`.

### 4.3 `ç` before consonants

Keep `ç` even where it sounds like `ş`: `açdy`, `göçjek`, `saçly`, `güýçli`, `içsem`.

### 4.4 Diminutives `-jyk/-jik/-jak/-jek`

Final `k` of a word of two or more syllables drops: `taýajyk`, `köýnejik`, `bölejik`.

## 5. Dropped vowels

### 5.1 `agyz → agzy`

Two-syllable words with short vowels, ending in `z l n r s ş` with a narrow second vowel, lose that vowel before a vowel-initial suffix:

- `agyz → agzy`, `ogul → ogly`, `bagyr → bagry`, `burun → burny`, `köwüş → köwşi`
- loans too: `asyl → asly`, `pasyl → pasly`, `ylym → ylmy`, `nesil → nesli`
- Wrong → right: `agyzy → agzy`, `oguly → ogly`, `ogulynyň → oglunyň`
- The person of the ending does not change when the vowel drops. Check it, because the short forms look alike:

  | | his/her | his/her (genitive) | your (sen) | your (genitive) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | `ogul` | `ogly` | `oglunyň` | `ogluň` | `ogluňyň` |
  | `agyz` | `agzy` | `agzynyň` | `agzyň` | `agzyňyň` |
  | `burun` | `burny` | `burnunyň` | `burnuň` | `burnuňyň` |
  | `howuz` | `howzy` | `howzunyň` | `howzuň` | `howzuňyň` |

  When the dropped vowel was `u` (`ogul`, `burun`, `howuz`), the next suffix vowel is `u`, not `y`: `oglunyň`, `howzunda`, `howzuny` (not `howzynda`, `howzyny`). Confirmed by a native speaker. After a dropped `y` (`agyz`) it stays `y`: `agzynyň`.

  `Ogluň toýy` means "your son's wedding"; a woman talking about her own son's wedding in the third person is `oglunyň toýy`. Found in a test where `ogulynyň` became `ogluň`.
- Nouns in `-ýyş/-ýiş`: `ýaşaýyş → ýaşaýşy`, `sözleýiş → sözleýşi`
- Not every word drops the vowel: `düwün → düwüni` (not `düwni`). Confirmed by a native speaker; the word list in `scripts/sozluk/` has `düwni` and is wrong here.

Check unusual words in the dictionary; the rule has conditions that vary.

### 5.2 Vowels that must stay in writing

- Suffix vowels swallowed in speech are written: `golaýymyz`, `baglaryňyz`, `ýorganlaryny`.
- Verb stems in `-a/-e`, `-la/-le` keep the vowel before `-ýar/-ýär`: `ýaşaýar` (not ýaşyýar), `barlaýar`, `gözleýär`, `goraýar`, `ýatmaýar`.
- No helping vowel in final clusters: `wagt`, `howp`, `süýt`, `berk`, `üst`.

## 6. Loanwords

- Russian and international roots are written as in Russian, in Turkmen letters: `traktor`, `institut`, `žurnal`.
- Older assimilated loans have fixed Turkmen forms: `gazet` (not gazeta), `maşyn`, `minut`, `sekunt`, `fabrik`, `äpişge`, `çäýnek`, `penjek`, `bedre`.
- Doubled final consonants become single: `metal`, `kilogram`, `proges`.
- Russian adjective endings are dropped or replaced: `aktual sorag`, `original iş`, `akademik`, `pedagogik`.
- Loans in `-ý` drop it before a suffix (`wožatyda`), except `muzeý`, `liseý`, `maý` (`muzeýde`, `maýda`).
- `Baku`, `Tartu` take `w` before vowel suffixes: `Bakuwda`, `Bakuwdan`.

## 7. Hyphens, abbreviations, numbers

- Ordinary suffixes attach directly: `Merediň`, `öýde`, `ýazdyk`.
- Particles take a hyphen: `aldy-da`, `geldi-de`, `bolsa-da`, `Oraz-a`, `Durdy-ha`.
- Abbreviations: capitals, no full stops, hyphen before a suffix that matches how the last letter is read: `ABŞ-nyň`, `BMG-niň`, `SSSR-iň`.
- Ordinals in digits: hyphen + `-njy/-nji`, chosen by how the last word of the number is read: `6-njy`, `9-njy`, `10-njy`, `3-nji`, `25-nji oktýabr`, `2026-njy ýyl`. Not `6-nci`, not `9.`.
- `hem` merged into the previous word (`menem`, `senem`) is informal; in news and documents write `hem` separately.

## 8. Compound words

- Written together: fixed compounds (`Gündogar`, `günorta`, `ýolgörkeziji`, `Daşhowuz`), words with `bi-`, `bed-/bet-`, `nä-` (`biedep`, `betbagt`, `nätanyş`), and `kä-` words (`käwagt`, `kämahal`, `käbir`).
- `kä` as a repeated conjunction is separate: `Kä bulut gelýär, kä gün çykýar.`
- Hyphenated: paired and repeated words (`aýry-aýry`, `sorag-bisorag`), and *(usage)* pairs of related or opposite words (`ata-ene`, `gije-gündiz`, `uly-kiçi`, `ene-ata`).
- Free noun phrases are separate *(usage)*: `Daşary işler ministrligi`.
- For any other compound, check the dictionary instead of guessing.

## 9. Capital letters

- Names, surnames, pen names: `Magtymguly Pyragy` (two words, no hyphen), `Mämmetweli Kemine`.
- Geographic names, streets, squares: only the proper part is capitalized: `Aşgabat şäheri`, `Mary welaýaty`, `Murgap derýasy`, `Azatlyk meýdançasy`. Not `Aşgabat Şäheri`.
- Derived words are lowercase: `aşgabatly`, `garagumly`. Forms with `-daky/-däki`, `-syz/-siz` stay capitalized: `Aşgabatdaky mekdep`.
- Heavenly bodies: `Ýer`, `Aý`, `Gün`, `Mars`.
- Holidays and events: first word only: `Täze ýyl`, `Birinji maý`; with a digit, the next word is capitalized: `8-nji Mart`, `9-njy Maý`.
- International organizations: each word except `we`: `Birleşen Milletler Guramasy`, `Gyzyl Haç we Gyzyl Ýarymaý Jemgyýeti`.
- Stylistic capitals: `Watan`, polite `Siz`.
- *(usage)* State bodies: `Türkmenistanyň Mejlisi`, `Türkmenistanyň Ministrler Kabineti`, `Türkmenistanyň Bilim ministrligi`, `Türkmenistanyň Prezidenti`. Months and weekdays are lowercase: `oktýabr`, `duşenbe`. Titles of documents go in «» with the first word capitalized. Follow the newsroom's own style book where it differs.

## 10. Line breaks

Break between syllables (`baý-dak`, `de-mir-çi`); never split a one-syllable word (`sport`, `tekst`), never leave a single vowel on a line (`a-dam` is wrong), never split an abbreviation.

## 11. Checklist

1. Any `c q v x ı ğ ñ`, or an apostrophe before a suffix?
2. `y` vs `ý`: `ýol`, `ýyl`, `gelýär`, `baý`.
3. `ä` in front suffixes: `-ýär`, `-räk`, `-däki`.
4. Suffix harmony with the last vowel of the stem: `kitaplar`, `öýlerde`, `kompýuterler`.
5. `k p t ç → g b d j` where required (`kitaby`, `agajy`), not after short one-syllable stems (`itiň`) or `-at/-et` loans (`hökümeti`).
6. Dropped vowels: `agzy`, `ogly`, `burny`, `ýaşaýşy`.
7. `o`/`ö` only in the first syllable of simple words: `oglan`, `börek`.
8. Hyphen with particles, abbreviations and ordinals: `bolsa-da`, `ABŞ-nyň`, `6-njy`.
9. Generic geographic word lowercase: `Aşgabat şäheri`.
10. Assimilated loans in their Turkmen form: `gazet`, `maşyn`, `minut`, `metal`.
