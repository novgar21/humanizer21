# Turkmen punctuation, word choice and text flow

Summarized in our own words from the syntax, punctuation and lexicon pages of enedilim.com. Points marked *(usage)* are standard Turkmen press practice that those pages do not cover.

Contents: 1 word order · 2 homogeneous members · 3 address, response words, interjections, introductory words · 4 insertions and clarifications · 5 compound and complex sentences · 6 direct speech and quotations · 7 dialogue · 8 the dash · 9 loanwords and native words · 10 register and synonyms · 11 old and new words · 12 building a text

## 1. Word order

- Subject, then objects and adverbials, then the predicate. The verb comes last: `Mugallym okuwçylara täze sapagy düşündirdi.`
- Modifiers go before what they modify: `uly stadion`, `çalt we oňat tikýär`.
- Usually time before place: `Biz ertir mekdepde ýygnak geçireris.`
- The word right before the verb carries the focus.
- Subordinate and converb clauses go before the main clause.
- A noun or adverb after the verb (`Ol geldi düýn.`) reads as translated unless it is deliberate colloquial speech. Write `Ol düýn geldi.`

## 2. Homogeneous members

Same role, same question, same head word. Without a conjunction, separate with commas: `Ol okaýar, ýazýar, surat çekýär.`

| Conjunction | Comma | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `we` (once, before the last item) | none | `Aman, Jemal we Oraz geldi.` |
| `hem` (single) | none | `çalt hem düşnükli` |
| `-da/-de` after a verb | hyphen, then comma | `Oturdy-da, ýazmaga başlady.` |
| `emma`, `ýöne` | before | `Ýagyş ýagdy, emma sowuk däldi.` |
| `weli`, `welin` | after (they follow the first part) | `Çalt ýöredik weli, gijä galdyk.` |
| `ýogsa`, `bolmasa` | before | `Tiz bol, ýogsam gijä galarys.` |
| `ýa` (single) | none | `çaý ýa kofe` |
| `ýa ... ýa-da`, `kä ... käte`, `ne ... ne-de`, `hem ... hem` | before the repeated one | `ne ýazdy, ne-de jaň etdi` |

A generalizing word before the list takes a colon, after the list a dash:

- `Üç dersden synag tabşyrdyk: matematika, fizika, himiýa.`
- `Maýa, Jennet, Sona – üçüsi bir synpda okaýar.`

## 3. Words outside the sentence structure

All of these are set off with commas.

**Address.** `Aýna, bärik gel.` / `Men, dostum, saňa ynanýaryn.` / `Sag boluň, hormatly mugallym.` A strong call at the start may take `!`, and the next word is capitalized: `Eziz watandaşlar!`

**Response words.** `hawa`, `ýok`, `bolýar`, `dogry`: `Hawa, men gelerin.`

**Interjections.** `waý`, `eý`, `ah`, `wah`: `Waý, nähili gowy!`

**Introductory words**, commas on both sides when mid-sentence:

| Meaning | Words |
|---|---|
| certainty | `şübhesiz`, `elbetde`, `hakykatdanam`, `dogrudan`, `şeksiz` |
| whose opinion | `meniňçe`, `biziňçe`, `onuň pikiriçe`, `aýtmagyna görä`, `habar berlişi ýaly`, `bellenilişi ýaly` |
| summing up | `diýmek`, `şeýlelikde`, `umuman`, `netijede`, `jemläp aýdanymyzda` |
| uncertainty | `ähtimal`, `belki`, `megerem`, `çaky` |
| order, examples *(usage)* | `birinjiden`, `ikinjiden`, `ilki bilen`, `meselem`, `aýratyn hem` |

`Meniňçe, bu dogry çözgüt.` / `Ertir, belki, ýagyş ýagar.` One introductory word per sentence is plenty; opening every paragraph with `Şeýlelikde,` or `Umuman,` is a machine habit.

## 4. Insertions and clarifications

- An inserted clause is set off with commas, dashes or brackets: `Düýn, seniň habaryň bardyr, mekdepde baýramçylyk boldy.`
- *(usage)* Brackets for decoded abbreviations and short explanations: `BMG (Birleşen Milletler Guramasy)`, `Türkmenistanyň Mejlisi (parlamenti)`.
- *(usage)* A title or profession directly before a name takes no comma: `ýazyjy B. Kerbabaýew`, `Aşgabat şäheri`.
- *(usage)* A clarification after the noun: comma or dash: `Biz paýtagtymyz – Aşgabat barada gürrüň etdik.`
- *(usage)* Comma before `ýagny`, `meselem`, `hususan-da`, `şol sanda`; `meselem` usually takes one after as well: `Käbir miweler, meselem, alma we üzüm, bu ýerde köp ösýär.`

## 5. Compound and complex sentences

**Coordinate clauses.**

- No conjunction: commas: `Gün çykdy, guşlar saýrady, oba oýandy.`
- `we` / `hem` between two clauses: no comma: `Ýel öwüsdi we ýagyş ýagdy.`
- `-da/-de`: hyphen + comma: `Gün çykdy-da, töwerek ýagtyldy.`
- `emma`, `ýöne`: comma before; `weli`: comma after.
- *(usage)* Colon when the second clause explains the first: `Bir zady bilýärin: ol hökman geler.` Dash for a sharp result: `Ýagyş ýagdy – ýollar batga boldy.`

**Subordinate clauses.** Put a comma between the dependent and main clause, except when the dependent part stands directly before the main predicate.

- `Men gelenimde, otagda hiç kim ýokdy.`
- `Gün biz gelenimizden soň ýaşdy.` (no comma)

| Type | Marker | Example |
|---|---|---|
| time | `-anda/-ende`, `-andan soň`, `-ýança`, `-mazdan öň` | `Işden gelenimde, ejem garaşýardy.` |
| condition | `-sa/-se` | `Wagtyň bolsa, jaň et.` |
| concession | `-sa hem`, `-sa-da` | `Ýadasa-da, işini dowam etdi.` |
| cause | `diýip`, `sebäpli`, `üçin` | `Ýagyş ýaganlygy sebäpli, oýun yza süýşürildi.` |
| comparison | `ýaly`, `kimin` | `Öňküsi ýaly, bu gezek hem ýeňdik.` |
| sequence | `-yp/-ip` | `Ol öýe gelip, nahar edindi.` |

- `sebäbi`, `çünki` introduce a following cause, with a comma before: `Ol gelmedi, sebäbi syrkawdy.` *(usage)* Native style often prefers the preposed cause: `Syrkaw bolandygy sebäpli, ol gelmedi.` A `sebäbi` in every other sentence sounds translated from Russian "потому что".
- Correlative pairs *(usage)*: `Kim zähmet çekse, şol hormat gazanar.`
- A short converb right before the verb takes no comma: `Ol ylgap geldi.`

## 6. Direct speech and quotations

The site requires double quotation marks. Turkmen print and state media use `«...»`, with `„...“` for a quote inside a quote *(usage)*. Use `«»` unless the user's text consistently uses another style.

**Author first, then the quote:** colon, quote with a capital letter; Turkmen usually adds a verb of saying after it:

- `Ol: «Ertir gelerin» diýdi.`
- `Şol wagt Çarynyň: «Gurgunmysyň, inim?» diýip, sesi eşidildi.`

**Quote first, then `diýip`/`diýdi`:** no comma between the closing mark and `diýip` (unlike Russian `», — сказал`). A comma after `diýip` only if another word stands before the predicate:

- `«Gelýärin» diýip, Aýna ýylgyrdy.`
- `«Sen geldiňmi?» diýip sorady.`

**`diýen` + noun:** no colon, no comma: `«Belki, ol geler» diýen pikir hemmäni begendirdi.`, `«Ýaşlar – geljegimiz» diýen şygar`.

**A quote built into the sentence** starts lowercase and may take Turkmen endings.

**Omitted words:** `...`; source in brackets after the quote.

**The quote's own punctuation is the speaker's.** Change only the quotation marks. A dash, comma or exclamation mark inside a quote stays, even where this file would advise another mark in your own text; the dash rule in section 8 does not apply inside quotes. In a translation, keep each mark of the original quote where Turkmen allows it: `«Мы ждём всех — от первоклассников до ветеранов»` → `«Biz hemmä garaşýarys – birinji synp okuwçylaryndan weteranlara çenli»`, not a comma or a rebuilt sentence. The same goes for a quoted greeting with its set wishes (`size berk jan saglyk, ...`).

**Indirect speech**, the normal news register:

- `-dygyny / -jakdygyny` + `aýtdy / belledi / nygtady / habar berdi`: `Ol ertir geljekdigini aýtdy.`
- `X-yň belleýşi ýaly, ...`: `Ministriň belleýşi ýaly, iş dowam edýär.`
- `barada` / `hakynda`: `Ol täze taslama barada gürrüň berdi.`

## 7. Dialogue

- Each turn on a new line, starting with a dash and a capital letter; no quotation marks.
- After the turn, a dash before the author's words; the turn's own `?` or `!` stays before the dash.
- After a statement followed by author's words, no full stop. Books differ on whether a comma goes there; pick one style and keep it.

```
Aman gapydan girdi:
— Salam, eje!
— Gel, oglum. Näme beýle gijä galdyň? — diýip, ejesi sorady.
— Ýol köp boldy — diýdi Aman.
```

## 8. The dash *(usage)*

Turkmen uses the dash for specific jobs:

- between a noun subject and a noun predicate with no copula: `Aşgabat – Türkmenistanyň paýtagty.`, `Kitap – bilim çeşmesi.` No dash with `-dyr` or an adjective/verb predicate: `Aşgabat Türkmenistanyň paýtagtydyr.`, `Howa gowy.`
- before a generalizing word after a list
- for an omitted word: `Aman mekdebe gitdi, Jeren – bazara.`
- in dialogue
- for ranges: `2020–2025-nji ýyllar`
- headlines and slogans: `Ýaşlar – geljegimiz`

A dash used as an all-purpose connector between clauses is a machine habit. Use a comma, colon or full stop instead. This applies to your own text only, never inside a quote (section 6).

## 9. Loanwords and native words

Three layers:

1. **Old Arabic and Persian loans** are fully Turkmen. Never replace them: `kitap`, `mekdep`, `mugallym`, `ylym`, `döwlet`, `hökümet`, `wagt`, `sagat`, `dünýä`, `şäher`, `jemgyýet`, `maslahat`, `häkim`, `welaýat`.
2. **Russian and international loans** keep their root, written in Turkmen letters: `kompýuter`, `telefon`, `internet`, `prezident`, `programma`, `tehnologiýa`, `stansiýa`, `informasiýa`, `reklama`. Russian я → `ýa`, ю → `ýu`, ц → `s`, `-ция` → `-siýa`, `-ия` → `-iýa`.
3. **Current formal and state media usage prefers the established native or older word** where one exists *(usage)*:

|---|---|---|
| `welaýat` | oblast | province |
| `etrap` | raýon | district |
| `häkimlik` | administrasiýa | local administration |
| `geňeş` | sowet | council |
| `kazyýet` | sud | court |
| `kärhana` | predpriýatiýe | enterprise |
| `ulgam` | sistema | system |
| `maglumat` | informasiýa | information |
| `aragatnaşyk` | swýaz | communications |
| `ulag` | transport | transport |
| `uçar` | samolýot | airplane |
| `tikuçar` | wertolýot | helicopter |
| `kitaphana` | biblioteka | library |
| `hassahana` | bolnisa | hospital |
| `lukman` | wraç | doctor |
| `talyp` | student | university student |
| `ýokary okuw mekdebi` | WUZ | university, college |
| `ykdysadyýet` | ekonomika | economy |
| `syýasat` | politika | politics, policy |
| `sanly ulgam`, `sanlylaşdyrmak` | sifrowoý, sifrowizasiýa | digital, digitalization |

| `telekeçi` | biznesmen | entrepreneur |

Keep international words that have no established Turkmen equivalent: `prezident`, `parlament`, `ministr`, `internet`, `kompýuter`, `programma`, `tehnologiýa`, `innowasiýa`, `sport`, `teatr`, `konsert`. Inventing a native word where none is in use produces odd text.

## 10. Register and synonyms

| Everyday | Formal / news | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `gowy` | `oňat`, `ýagşy` | good |
| `aýtmak` | `bellemek`, `nygtamak`, `beýan etmek` | say, note, stress |
| `etmek` | `amala aşyrmak`, `durmuşa geçirmek`, `ýerine ýetirmek` | do, carry out |
| `başlamak` | `badalga bermek`, `ýola goýmak` | start, launch |
| `bermek` | `gowşurmak`, `tabşyrmak` | give, hand over |
| `gürrüň` | `söhbetdeşlik`, `gepleşikler` | talk, negotiations |
| `kömek` | `ýardam`, `goldaw` | help, support |
| `köp` | `ençeme`, `birnäçe` | many, several |

- News uses the formal column, but rotate: `aýtdy`, `belledi`, `nygtady`, `habar berdi`. The same verb five times in a row is a tell.
- Do not drift into poetic words (`gurt`, `mekan`, `görmegeý`) in neutral prose.
- `beýik` is "great, lofty", not a plain "big"; `möhüm` is "important", not "large".
- Formal verbs used where nothing formal happens (`amala aşyrmak` for "did") are padding.

### Words that look interchangeable but are not

Machine Turkmen often picks the wrong one of two close words, and neither `barla.py` nor a spelling dictionary will catch it, because both words are spelled correctly.

| Word | Means | Use for | Not for |
|---|---|---|---|
| `howuz` (`howzy`) | a small, built basin of water, open or indoor | a swimming pool (`ýüzüş howzy`), a fountain basin | a lake or reservoir |
| `howdan` | a body of water in the open, a reservoir or pond | a reservoir, a pond in nature | a pool in a sports complex |
| `basseýn` (`derýanyň basseýni`) | the area a river drains | `Murgap derýasynyň basseýni` (Russian `бассейн реки`) | a swimming pool |
| `derýa` and `kanal` | a river, and a channel dug by people | `Murgap derýasy`; `suwaryş kanaly` | each other: check which one the place is |

Confirmed by a native speaker after tests in which a 25-metre pool in a sports complex came out as `ýüzüş howdany`, and the river Murgap as `Murgap kanaly`. Add further pairs here as they turn up.

## 11. Old and new words

- Soviet terms are now historical or replaced: `kolhoz` → `daýhan birleşigi`, `raýon` → `etrap`, `oblast` → `welaýat`, `sowet` → `geňeş`. Use them only when writing about the past.
- Current state terms *(usage)*: `Halk Maslahaty`, `Mejlis`, `häkimlik`, `arçyn`, `geňeş`, `Ministrler Kabineti`, `Garaşsyzlyk`, `Bitaraplyk`.
- Economy and technology: `telekeçilik`, `salgyt`, `karz`, `maýa goýum`, `hususy pudak`, `sanly ykdysadyýet`, `elektron hökümet`, `maglumat tehnologiýalary`, `programma üpjünçiligi`, `smartfon`.
- Month names: use the international ones (`ýanwar`, `fewral`, ...). The names used from 2002 to 2008 are no longer current.

## 12. Building a text

- A text is a set of sentences linked in meaning and grammar. Two ways to link:
  - **chain:** the next sentence picks up a word from the previous one, repeated, replaced by a pronoun (`bu`, `şol`, `ol`) or by a synonym: `Obada täze mekdep açyldy. Mekdepde 500 okuwçy okar.`
  - **parallel:** neighbouring sentences share a structure, as in a description: `Çagalar oýnaýardy, ulular gürrüň edýärdi.`
- A paragraph holds one part of the content. Start a new one when the topic, time, speaker or mood changes.
- Converbs link actions more naturally than `we ... we ... we`:
  - weak: `Ol geldi we oturdy we gürrüň berdi.`
  - better: `Ol gelip oturdy-da, gürrüň berip başlady.`
- Long converb chains are normal in official prose; three or four converbs before the main verb is the readable limit.
- After the first full mention, shorten: `Türkmenistanyň Prezidenti` → `Döwlet Baştutanymyz` or `Prezident`.
- Calques to avoid *(usage)*: `boýunça` for every Russian "по"; `bolup durýar` for every "является" (often a plain `-dyr` reads better); passive with `tarapyndan` where an active sentence is clearer; chains of four or more genitives.
- Text types: narration in `-dy/-di` (and `-ypdyr` for reported facts); description in `-ýardy` with parallel clauses; reasoning with `meniňçe`, `diýmek`, `-sa`, `-dygy üçin`.
